1 · ANNUAL ENERGY TO AVERAGE POWER

Exact by definition — annual energy divided by the hours in the year. It is the only figure on this page that carries no assumption, and the least useful on its own, because no network is ever sized for its average.

2 · AVERAGE TO PEAK, AT A LOAD FACTOR YOU STATE

Peak = average ÷ load factor. The load factor is the entire assumption, so it is never defaulted — you set it, and it travels with the answer. A system with unmanaged heat pumps and one with smart-charged vehicles are different numbers.

3 · NESO'S PUBLISHED 2050 PATHWAYS, MEASURED

The honest direction of travel: where a peak is published, derive the load factor from it rather than assuming one. Note the third row — Hydrogen Evolution carries the largest annual demand and a lower peak than Electric Engagement. No single national peak-to-average ratio can describe both, which is why scaling today's ratio forward is wrong.

5 · DISPLACED FUEL TO ELECTRICITY

Useful output is conserved, not fuel energy. Burning fuel wastes most of it; a heat pump moves more heat than the energy it draws. Skipping this step — treating primary energy as future electrical load — is the commonest error in electrification arithmetic.
