## ERCOT / Texas Winter Grid Failure Studies

Texas provides an important contrast to the Iberian blackout. While the Iberian event highlights voltage control, reactive power, inverter behaviour and real time observability, Texas highlights cold weather resilience, fuel security, market design, reserve adequacy and the risks of electrical isolation.

### 2021 Texas Power Crisis, Winter Storm Uri

The February 2021 Texas power crisis is a key modern case study in weather driven grid failure. Extreme cold increased electricity demand while generation and fuel systems failed across multiple technologies. ERCOT ordered controlled outages to prevent wider collapse. The event exposed weaknesses in winterisation, gas supply resilience, reserve planning and the state's limited ability to import emergency power.

Useful study themes:

1. Weatherisation of generation and fuel infrastructure
2. ERCOT grid isolation and limited interconnection
3. Energy only market incentives and reserve adequacy
4. Gas and electricity system interdependence
5. Load shedding, public harm and infrastructure resilience

### 2011 Southwest Cold Weather Event

The February 2011 event was a direct warning before 2021. Cold weather caused generation outages and fuel supply problems, leading to rolling blackouts. FERC and NERC subsequently recommended stronger winterisation and cold weather preparedness.

FERC and NERC report on outages and curtailments during the Southwest cold weather event of February 1 to 5 2011:

https://www.ferc.gov/media/report-outages-and-curtailments-during-southwest-cold-weather-event-february-1-5-2011

### 1989 Texas Freeze

The December 1989 freeze provides an earlier warning that Texas power infrastructure was vulnerable to prolonged cold weather. The repetition of similar failure modes across 1989, 2011 and 2021 makes Texas a useful long term grid governance case study.
