# The SCADA palette: one colour, one meaning, everywhere

Shared by GridAtlas, the line wafer, the Spider sandbox and Pipeline News, so that a colour learned
on one screen means the same thing on the next. A control room cannot afford a colour that means
two things, and neither can this estate.

| meaning | colour | hex | where it already is |
|---|---|---|---|
| the record, as measured: dust, nodes, text | x-ray grey-blue / white | `#c9d3e6` / `#ffffff` | the wafer's dust; GridAtlas substations at zoom-out |
| the instrument: frames, headings, graticules, buttons | cyan | `#2ee6e6` | GridAtlas chrome, GRID / SUBS / LAYERS |
| energised, operational, lit by a query | green | `#22ee77` | GridAtlas operational projects; a lit repository or period on the wafer |
| attention: new, under construction, a boundary | amber | `#ffb000` | GridAtlas MW figures and "under construction"; new code on the wafer |
| selected, the thing under the hand | magenta | `#ff2bd6` | GridAtlas distribution substations; the wafer's crosshair |
| the ground | near-black navy | `#090c13` | both |

Voltage colours on GridAtlas (400 kV blue, 275 kV red, 132 kV green, lower voltages violet) are a
second, separate legend that belongs to networks and is only ever used on a network.

Rules: nothing is coloured for decoration. A shade must stand for a stated, invertible quantity
(law L20). A lattice line must mean something: the wafer's rings are month boundaries because
radius is time, and it has no spokes because angle means nothing there.
