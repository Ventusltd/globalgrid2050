# Executive Summary

This guidance sets out a structured engineering framework for preparing Employer Requirements for large scale solar photovoltaic power plants.

Its purpose is to help developers, investors, owners, designers, EPC contractors and technical advisers identify the electrical evidence needed before a project moves from design into procurement, construction, commissioning or long term operation.

Large scale solar projects are not simply collections of modules, inverters, cables, transformers and switchgear. They are distributed electrical infrastructure systems where DC arrays, low voltage AC collector systems, medium voltage networks, substations, earthing systems, surge protection, power electronics, communication systems and commissioning evidence must be assessed together.

The guidance is organised into linked technical sections so that each topic can be reviewed independently while remaining part of the wider system. It covers system level electrical behaviour, low voltage AC collector systems, DC string and cable systems, electromagnetic behaviour, parallel conductors, magnetic forces, earthing, insulation coordination, harmonics, transformerless inverter behaviour, surge protection, DC leakage, corrosion, cable materials, water blocking, fault studies, cable thermal rating, protection coordination, substation interfaces, cable routing, engineering review, long term infrastructure risk, observed failure modes and practical risk reduction.

A central principle of this work is that a single line diagram is not a complete design unless it is supported by validated studies, equipment ratings, protection settings, cable calculations, earthing philosophy, surge protection coordination, commissioning evidence and as built records.

The DC section is a core part of the guidance. It treats the photovoltaic DC field as a live outdoor electrical network with its own voltage stress, current behaviour, connector population, cable ageing, insulation life, leakage risk, capacitance, inductance, transient behaviour, surge exposure, earthing interaction and maintenance burden. This includes requirements for string mapping, cold voltage checks, bifacial current assumptions, cable material life, connector compatibility, reverse current assessment, duct congestion, thermal pinch points, Class Two insulation, floating DC systems, DC leakage, corrosion, common mode voltage, transformerless inverter interaction, potential induced degradation and DC side electromagnetic behaviour.

The low voltage AC collector system section addresses a risk layer often underestimated in string inverter solar plants. It requires clear definition of inverter feeder topology, switchboards, MCCBs, fault levels, busbar ratings, cable grouping, thermal derating, protection coordination, harmonics, isolation, earthing and commissioning deliverables.

The wider guidance extends the same evidence based approach across earthing systems, surge protection, insulation coordination, harmonics, fault studies, cable thermal performance, substation interfaces and long term infrastructure reliability.

The purpose is not to prescribe a single technology, supplier or topology. The purpose is to require proof. A project should be able to demonstrate that its electrical design is coherent, coordinated, maintainable, safe and aligned with the conditions under which it will operate for decades.

This guidance should therefore be used as a review framework, drafting aid, technical checklist and investor education tool. It is intended to improve clarity at the point where project decisions become commercially and technically difficult to reverse.
