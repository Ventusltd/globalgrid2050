## 4.11. Medium Voltage Collection System Interface

<p style="color:#b00020; font-weight:700;">Work in progress guidance note. This section is not a final project specific Employer's Requirement, design specification or construction instruction. Some concepts listed below may not be required on every project and may only apply where justified by project size, topology, grid connection requirements, equipment selection, network operator requirements or site conditions. This guidance must be reviewed by suitably competent people with relevant experience and qualifications before use on any live project.</p>

The low voltage AC collector system should not be treated as complete unless its interface with the medium voltage collection system is fully defined, studied and documented.

The medium voltage collection system should be treated as a critical electrical infrastructure layer of the solar plant. It should not be treated as a simple onward connection between inverter transformers and the grid substation.

The medium voltage system may include LV to MV transformers, 33kV or other MV cables, ring main units, circuit breakers, disconnectors, earthing switches, protection relays, current transformers, voltage transformers, cable terminations, cable screens, sheath voltage limiters, link boxes, earthing conductors, earthing transformers, neutral earthing resistors, harmonic filters, reactive power equipment, surge arresters, civil trenches, ducts, joint bays and commissioning test records.

The Contractor should provide a project specific medium voltage single line diagram showing how inverter stations, transformers, RMUs, feeders, normally open points, protection zones and the main grid interface are connected.

Where an open ring, radial network, closed ring or other topology is proposed, the Contractor should explain how the topology affects fault isolation, generation restoration, maintainability, protection grading, cable loading, switching safety and operational resilience.

The Contractor should identify and justify the transformer configuration used between the low voltage AC collector system and the medium voltage collection system. This should include transformer rating, voltage ratio, vector group, impedance, tap range, cooling method, protection devices, temperature monitoring, neutral earthing arrangement and fault withstand capability.

The Employer should not prescribe the transformer vector group, neutral earthing method or protection interface at concept stage unless the project has already been reviewed by competent specialists. The Contractor should provide a competent specialist design note explaining why the selected transformer arrangement is suitable for the project topology, inverter behaviour, earthing philosophy, earth fault detection, zero sequence current behaviour, harmonic behaviour, protection grading, insulation coordination and grid compliance.

The Contractor should identify whether an MV earthing transformer, neutral earthing resistor or other neutral earthing arrangement is required. The design should explain how earth fault current is limited, detected, cleared and coordinated with feeder protection, transformer protection, cable screen bonding, site earthing and grid interface requirements.

Medium voltage cable sizing should be verified using recognised engineering methods such as IEC 60287 or another accepted method. Cable sizing should not rely on catalogue ratings alone.

The Contractor should model the actual installation conditions, including route length, conductor material, conductor size, insulation type, screen construction, burial depth, duct sections, grouping, spacing, soil thermal resistivity, thermal backfill, ambient ground temperature, cable formation, cyclic solar loading, voltage drop, power loss and short circuit withstand.

The medium voltage cable schedule should state continuous current rating, cyclic rating where used, voltage drop, calculated losses, short circuit rating, termination requirements and installation assumptions.

For single core medium voltage cables, the Contractor should provide a written cable screen bonding design. The design should identify whether solid bonding, single point bonding, cross bonding, sectional bonding or another approved arrangement is proposed.

Solid bonding of long single core medium voltage cable routes can create circulating sheath currents and additional heating. It should not be used by default unless the Contractor proves that sheath losses, cable temperature, touch voltage, thermal derating and fault current return path are acceptable.

Where single point bonding is proposed, the Contractor should assess standing sheath voltage, transient sheath voltage, touch voltage and the need for sheath voltage limiters.

Where cross bonding is proposed, the Contractor should provide the major section layout, link box locations, sheath voltage limiter locations, screen transposition arrangement, earthing method and test procedure.

A separate earth continuity conductor should be provided unless the Contractor proves that the cable screen bonding system provides an adequate and safe fault current path under all credible operating and fault conditions.

Where sheath voltage limiters are used, the Contractor should provide ratings, locations, duty assumptions, earthing connections, inspection access and maintenance requirements. Link boxes should be suitable for the site environment, including water ingress risk, flooding risk, mechanical protection and future access.

The Contractor should include medium voltage cable capacitance and charging current in the load flow, voltage control and reactive power studies. The study should consider daytime operation, low export operation, night time energisation, open circuit feeders, lightly loaded feeders and abnormal switching states.

The Contractor should assess voltage rise, reactive power export, Ferranti effect, grid code limits, PPC reactive control capability and any requirement for shunt reactors, harmonic filters, damping equipment or other compensation equipment.

Harmonic filters should not be assumed required or unnecessary at concept stage. The Contractor should prove through harmonic assessment whether filtering, damping or reactive compensation equipment is required.

Where harmonic filters, reactors, damping equipment or other power quality equipment are required by study, manufacturer data, network operator requirement or grid compliance assessment, they should be treated as essential plant equipment. They should be shown on the single line diagram, included in the bill of materials, procured, installed, commissioned and verified before energisation or commercial operation.

Where harmonic filters or reactive compensation equipment are installed, the Contractor should provide protection, interlocking, metering, discharge, isolation, earthing, maintenance and commissioning requirements for that equipment.

A missing required harmonic filter should be treated as a design control failure before it is treated as a procurement failure.

The Contractor should define power quality monitoring at the medium voltage and point of connection interface, including CT and VT connections, harmonic monitoring, voltage monitoring, reactive power monitoring and event recording where required by the grid connection agreement, network operator or compliance process.

The Contractor should assess switching transient risk for the medium voltage collection system. The assessment should consider energisation of long cable circuits, transformer energisation, RMU operation, circuit breaker closing, open ring operation, temporary backfeed conditions and abnormal switching states.

Where required, the Contractor should evaluate controlled energisation procedures, point on wave switching, pre insertion resistors, surge arrester placement and operational restrictions.

The Contractor should assess ferroresonance risk where long cable capacitance may interact with transformer magnetising inductance, voltage transformers or single phase switching conditions.

Uncontrolled single pole switching of medium voltage transformer or cable circuits should not be permitted unless proven safe by competent specialist assessment.

All RMUs, circuit breakers, disconnectors, earthing switches and medium voltage switchgear should be rated for system voltage, continuous current, fault making current, short time withstand current, internal arc requirement and environmental conditions.

The Contractor should provide switchgear datasheets confirming the applicable standard, voltage rating, current rating, making capacity, breaking capacity, short time withstand, internal arc classification where required, interlocking, position indication, auxiliary switches, test access, earthing facilities, earth bar connections, padlocking provisions and operational labelling.

The Contractor should provide a complete medium voltage protection coordination study. The study should include feeder protection, transformer protection, harmonic filter protection where applicable, busbar protection where applicable, overcurrent protection, earth fault protection, sensitive earth fault protection, neutral voltage displacement protection, CT and VT selection, intertripping, communication assisted protection where used and loss of mains interface.

Protection settings should be coordinated so that faults are cleared selectively and within the thermal withstand limits of cables, terminations, transformers, harmonic equipment and switchgear.

The civil design should protect the electrical performance of the medium voltage system. The Contractor should provide trench drawings, cable spacing, thermal backfill specification, duct details, road crossing details, rail crossing details where applicable, watercourse crossing details, pulling tension limits, bend radius limits, joint bay details, link box locations and as built route survey requirements.

Poor trenching, poor backfill, excessive pulling tension, incorrect bend radius, flooded link boxes and damaged cable sheaths should be treated as electrical risks.

Where the medium voltage collection system interfaces with substation equipment, transformers, harmonic filters, BESS equipment, solar feeders, PPC controls, metering, protection or civil works, the Contractor should maintain an interface schedule identifying technical boundaries, information release dates, design freeze points, procurement responsibility, commissioning responsibility and evidence required before energisation.

No medium voltage design assumption should be treated as closed unless it is traceable through the single line diagram, study reports, procurement documents, installation records, protection settings, control settings and commissioning evidence.

The Contractor should provide a full medium voltage testing and commissioning plan before energisation. The plan should include factory acceptance testing, site acceptance testing, cable sheath testing, insulation resistance testing, VLF testing or approved alternative testing, tan delta testing where required, partial discharge testing where required, transformer ratio checks, transformer insulation checks, switchgear interlock checks, primary injection where required, secondary injection, relay trip testing, end to end protection testing, earthing system testing, thermographic inspection and post energisation monitoring.

DC high voltage testing of modern XLPE medium voltage cables should not be used unless specifically approved by the cable manufacturer and accepted by the project engineer.

The Contractor should not state that a test method is mandatory unless it is required by the applicable standard, manufacturer, network operator, lender, owner's engineer or project specification.

Where an MV, protection, harmonic, PPC, metering or grid compliance test fails, the Contractor should identify and remedy the root cause before retesting. Temporary settings or temporary modifications introduced only to pass a test should not be accepted unless they remain safe, documented and valid for the design life.

The minimum deliverables for the medium voltage collection system should include the medium voltage single line diagram, medium voltage collection topology description, transformer datasheets, RMU and switchgear datasheets, earthing transformer and neutral earthing resistor requirements where applicable, medium voltage cable schedules, medium voltage cable route drawings, thermal ampacity calculations, voltage drop and loss calculations, short circuit study, protection coordination study, earthing and bonding design report, cable screen bonding and sheath voltage limiter plan, load flow study including cable charging, reactive power and voltage control study, harmonic assessment, harmonic filter protection requirements where applicable, power quality monitoring requirements, switching transient assessment where required, ferroresonance assessment where required, PPC and inverter grid compliance evidence, interface schedule, civil trenching and route protection drawings, cable pulling and jointing records, VLF and tan delta test reports where used, commissioning records and as built cable route survey.

The Employer may reject any medium voltage design submission that does not provide sufficient evidence, calculations, manufacturer data, study results, test procedures, interface records and competent sign off.

The medium voltage system should be accepted only when the as built plant, installed equipment, protection settings, control settings and point of connection evidence match the approved studies.

The purpose of this guidance is not to prescribe every specialist design answer at concept stage. The purpose is to ensure that the Contractor proves the medium voltage design before hidden technical risk is locked into buried cable routes, transformer interfaces, protection settings, switching procedures and commissioning assumptions.
